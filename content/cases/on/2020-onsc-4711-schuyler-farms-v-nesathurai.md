# Schuyler Farms Limited v. Dr. Nesathurai

## 30-second read

An MOH issued an s. 22 class order governing isolation arrangements for migrant farm workers, including a limit of three workers per bunkhouse during isolation. HSARB removed the three-person requirement. The Divisional Court allowed the MOH's appeal and restored it, holding that the Board had mishandled the class nature of the order and the evidence, and had failed to give appropriate effect to the precautionary approach.

## Why this matters to an MOH/AMOH

This is one of the most practically important modern Ontario cases on **reasonable and probable grounds, class orders, evidence, uncertainty, and the precautionary principle**.

## Factual background

During the early COVID-19 pandemic, the local MOH issued a class order applying to migrant farm workers and farm operators. The order included isolation requirements and a three-person-per-bunkhouse limit intended to reduce transmission risk. Schuyler Farms challenged the order before HSARB. The Board removed the numerical occupancy limit while leaving other requirements in place. The MOH appealed to Divisional Court.

## Issues

1. What does “reasonable and probable grounds” require in the public health context?
2. How should HSARB assess an MOH's evidence supporting a class order?
3. What is the role of the precautionary principle where evidence is evolving or uncertain?
4. How does the class-wide nature of an order affect the necessity analysis?

## Decision

The Divisional Court restored the impugned requirement. It accepted that the public health standard associated with reasonable and probable grounds is materially different from a trial standard of proof and operates in a precautionary context. The court emphasized that reasonable risk-reduction action need not await scientific certainty.

## Reasoning

The court also found that HSARB had not properly appreciated the **class** character of the order. A class order may necessarily use a generalized rule rather than re-engineering each premises individually. The evidence about bunkhouse layout, shared facilities, proximity and the consequences of transmission had a rational connection to the occupancy requirement.
