# Oglaza v. J.A.K.K. Tuesdays Sports Pub Inc.

## 30-second read

After repeated and public non-compliance with an s. 22 order, the MOH sought a court order under HPPA s. 102 restraining further contravention. The Superior Court granted relief. The decision explains that a statutory injunction is not governed in exactly the same way as an ordinary private injunction: a clear statutory breach and the public interest in compliance are central, while irreparable harm and balance-of-convenience considerations have a more limited role.

## Why this matters to an MOH/AMOH

This is a practical Ontario **“we have issued the order; now what?”** case. It is particularly useful for planning escalation where education, inspection and the underlying order have not produced compliance.

## Factual background

The MOH issued an s. 22 order requiring a Kingston pub to comply with applicable COVID-19 rules or close. Evidence showed repeated breaches and public statements indicating an intention to continue defying the requirements. The MOH applied without notice for a restraining order under s. 102.

## Issues

1. What test applies to a statutory restraining order under s. 102?
2. Was there sufficiently clear evidence of contravention?
3. What scope of relief was proportionate to securing compliance?

## Decision

The court granted the restraining order.

## Reasoning

The court reviewed principles applicable to statutory injunctions: the applicant must establish the statutory breach on the applicable civil standard; proof of private-law irreparable harm is not ordinarily an independent prerequisite; there is no requirement to exhaust other remedies first; and there is a strong public interest in observance of the law.

At the same time, the court treated s. 102 as **preventive**, not punitive. Relief should be proportionate and no more restrictive than reasonably required to secure compliance. On the evidence, measures that included securing the premises were justified.
