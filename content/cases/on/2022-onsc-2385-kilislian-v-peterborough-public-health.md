# Kilislian v. Peterborough Public Health

## 30-second read

Following IPAC concerns at an endodontic clinic, Peterborough Public Health required the clinic to produce identifying/contact information for active and former patients during a defined look-back period. HSARB confirmed the s. 13 production order. The Divisional Court dismissed the clinic's appeal — but the **only ground pursued on that appeal was procedural fairness concerning the clinic's non-lawyer representative**, not the substantive merits of the public-health order.

## Why this matters to an MOH/AMOH

The underlying proceeding is a useful example of using HPPA authority to obtain information needed for an IPAC look-back and patient notification. The Divisional Court decision is also an important reminder to distinguish the substantive findings made by HSARB from the narrower issues actually decided on appeal. For MOHs/AMOHs, the case is therefore relevant both as an example of public-health practice and as a caution against overstating what a reviewing court has endorsed.

## Factual background

PPH investigated a complaint regarding sterilization practices at an endodontic clinic. Inspectors identified IPAC concerns and initially ordered the clinic closed. After remediation, the closure was rescinded. The MOH subsequently required production of a list of former and active patients for a defined period so that PPH could conduct a look-back. The clinic sought review before HSARB. HSARB confirmed the production order in File 19-HPP-0008.

## Issues

At HSARB, the central public-health dispute concerned the production order. On appeal to Divisional Court, however, the appellants relied only on an alleged breach of procedural fairness: they argued that HSARB should have excluded Dr. Kilislian's husband, who had acted as their non-lawyer agent.

## Decision

The Divisional Court dismissed the appeal.

## Reasoning

The court found no obligation on HSARB to investigate and remove a party's chosen non-lawyer representative on its own initiative in the circumstances, absent an objection or demonstrated procedural unfairness/prejudice.

The Court of Appeal later dismissed a motion for leave to appeal. The Supreme Court of Canada refused leave (SCC File 40454).
