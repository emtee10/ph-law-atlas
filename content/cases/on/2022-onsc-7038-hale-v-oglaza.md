# Hale v. Oglaza

## 30-second read

HSARB confirmed an MOH's s. 22 order requiring a Kingston pub to comply with COVID-19 requirements or close. The Divisional Court dismissed the appeal, finding no error in HSARB's interpretation/application of s. 22. The decision is useful less for a new test than for showing the **review chain**: MOH order → HSARB hearing under s. 44 → Divisional Court appeal under s. 46.

## Why this matters to an MOH/AMOH

When drafting an order, it is helpful to think forward to the questions HSARB would ask. *Hale* shows that the Board can scrutinize the statutory predicates for an s. 22 order and that its resulting decision then reaches Divisional Court through the statutory appeal route.

## Factual background

The MOH issued an s. 22 order after complaints and evidence of non-compliance at J.A.K.K. Tuesdays Sports Pub. The order required compliance with then-applicable COVID-19 rules or closure. Court proceedings under s. 102 followed when the order was defied. Separately, the recipients exercised their right to a hearing before HSARB. The Board confirmed the order in February 2022.

## Issues

On appeal, the appellants challenged HSARB's interpretation and application of s. 22 and advanced other arguments, including reliance on the federal Canadian Bill of Rights.

## Decision

The Divisional Court dismissed the appeal. 

## Reasoning

The court noted that HSARB had reviewed the three statutory conditions in s. 22(2) and concluded that the MOH had reasonable and probable grounds for the required findings. The court found no merit in the appellants' legal arguments and no reviewable error in the Board's treatment of s. 22.
